About Jackson Place

Between the Central District, Rainier Valley, and the I-90 greenbelt, Jackson Place is one of Seattle’s most connected communities.

Jackson Place is a vibrant, culturally diverse residential neighborhood located southeast of downtown Seattle. It is within walking distance of Pioneer Square, the stadium district, and Lake Washington.

The area has historically been working class, and from the 1920s through 1950s was part of Seattle’s vibrant jazz scene. Today it is the site of several schools, churches, and small and large businesses, as well as many single family homes, apartments, and town houses. When the I-90 interchange was updated in the late 1980s, the community suffered major disruptions, as many of its houses and lots were seized for the construction. Since that time it has continued to change with the opening of the Judkins Park light rail station, along with all of the housing that was constructed around the station.

About the Council

The Council is organized as a nonprofit, nonpartisan, public-benefit corporation under Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code and the Washington Nonprofit Corporation Act.

Its purposes are to:

  1. Represent and advocate for the interests of the Jackson Place community.
  2. Preserve the neighborhood’s history, diversity, and quality of life.
  3. Foster community connection and participation among residents and businesses.
  4. Work with government, civic, and business organizations for programs that benefit the community.
  5. Share information important to neighbors and cooperate with nearby councils and groups.

Neighborhood boundaries

The Council’s bylaws define the Jackson Place community as the area bounded by:

  • North — Jackson Street
  • East — the western boundary of Washington Middle School and Judkins Park
  • South — Interstate 90
  • West — Rainier Avenue South

If you live, own property, or run a business inside these boundaries, you are eligible to be a Member of the Council and to vote in its elections.
